A murder at the Vance Estate. Thirty editions of a 1920s newspaper, every one of them written in cipher. Break the code and the story moves.

Chronicle Cryptogram is a substitution-cipher puzzle dressed as a vintage broadsheet. Tap a mark, type a letter, watch the quote take shape. No math — just English and a sharp eye for pattern.

This is a thirty-edition serial, and you read it at your own pace. Break the Morning Edition and the Night Extra drops — a harder second cipher with split glyphs for E, T, and A. Solve an edition and the next one lands on your desk. No clock on the wall telling you to come back tomorrow.

THE CASE ON YOUR DESK

A murder at the Vance Estate opens the run. Detective Elias Thorne leads the file. Beatrice Vance, the widow. Clara Vance, the missing heir. Arthur Sterling, the partner. Dr. Aris Blackwood, the physician. Reginald, the butler. Archibald Vance, the victim.

Each solved edition unlocks a case-file fragment — witness notes, dossiers, and quotes pulled from the cipher you just broke. Morning and evening ciphers feed different threads. By the thirtieth edition you should know who had motive, who lied, and who Thorne is still chasing.

HOW AN EDITION WORKS

• Morning Edition — one glyph per letter. Frequency counting works cleanly.
• Night Extra — unlocks after you decode the morning paper. E, T, and A each get two glyphs. Same quote, tougher rules.
• The timer runs from your first guess until the edition is solved. Post your time if you want bragging rights.
• Three letter checks and three hints per edition, shared across both papers. Use them when you are stuck, not when you are showing off.

TOOLS ON THE DESK

• Cipher Handbook — six briefings: single-letter words, frequency, short words, apostrophes, doubles, and how night rules differ.
• Glyph Tally — repeating marks counted busiest-first, with coaching on E, T, A, O, I, and N.
• Primer coaching — nudges tied to the same tactics the handbook teaches.
• Zoom and Clear Letters — resize crowded tiles or wipe guesses and start over. The clock keeps running.

Start on the Codebreaker's Primer, a training cipher with the rules spelled out, before the case opens.
